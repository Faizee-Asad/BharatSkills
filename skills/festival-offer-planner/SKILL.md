# Festival Offer Planner

For Indian shops, showrooms, salons, restaurants, and online sellers who want a
planned festival campaign instead of a last-minute "SALE" banner.

## When to use this skill
- "Diwali par kya offer rakhu?" / "Festival sale plan banana hai"
- "Eid / Rakhi / Onam ke liye offer aur message chahiye"
- "Festival me sales kaise badhau?" — planning, stock, messages
- "Customers ko festival greeting bhejna hai, offer ke saath"

## What you need from the user
- Business type and what they sell
- Which festival (and city/state — regional festivals differ)
- Their margin comfort: how much discount or what freebie is actually possible
- Where they will promote: WhatsApp list, Instagram, shop banner, local groups

## The Indian festival calendar (use the ones that fit your customers)
- **Diwali / Dhanteras** — the biggest sales season for most businesses
- **Navratri / Dussehra / Durga Puja** — strong in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Bengal
- **Eid-ul-Fitr (after Ramzan) and Eid-ul-Adha** — clothing, food, gifting, services
- **Raksha Bandhan** — gifts, sweets, clothing, jewellery
- **Onam** (Kerala), **Pongal / Makar Sankranti** (Tamil Nadu / harvest belt)
- **Ganesh Chaturthi** (Maharashtra), **Karwa Chauth** (North), **Akshaya Tritiya** (gold)
- **Christmas / New Year**, **Independence Day / Republic Day** sales
- **Wedding season** (roughly Nov–Feb and Apr–Jun) — not a festival, but the second
  biggest buying season for clothing, jewellery, beauty, catering and venues

Festival dates shift every year (lunar calendars). Never state a date from memory in
a customer message — check the current year's date and mark it `verify before use`
in your own plan.

## Workflow

### 1. Plan backwards from the festival day (start 2–3 weeks early)
- **T-21 to T-14:** decide the offer, check/order stock, get banner/poster designed
- **T-7:** first announcement — "coming soon" teaser on WhatsApp status + Instagram
- **T-3:** full offer message to your customer list; shop display ready
- **T-1 to festival day:** reminder + last-day urgency (only if the offer really ends)
- **T+1 to T+3:** thank-you message, ask for reviews/photos, note what sold best

### 2. Design an honest offer the margin can carry
Pick ONE clear main offer; confusion kills sales:
- Flat % off on a category ("Sarees par 20% off")
- Fixed combos ("Kurta + Pyjama set ₹999") — easy to understand, moves stock
- Freebie with a threshold ("₹2,000 ki khareed par mehndi free" — salon example)
- Gift-ready add-on: free gift packing / delivery during the festival week

Rules:
- ❌ Never inflate the price first and then show a fake big discount. Customers
  compare, and misleading pricing is also against consumer-protection rules.
- ❌ Never copy a big brand's offer if your margin cannot carry it — a small honest
  offer that you profit from beats a loss-making copy.
- ✅ Write the offer in one line a child can repeat: kya milega, kitne me, kab tak.
- ✅ Keep a small margin buffer for bargaining — Indian festival shopping includes it.

### 3. Send the messages (templates below)
Use the `whatsapp-business-writer` skill's tone guidance with these. Send greetings
even to customers who may not buy — festivals are relationship season first.

### 4. Close the loop after the festival
- Thank every buyer personally on WhatsApp (template below).
- Write down: best-selling item, total festival sales, what stock is left.
- This one page of notes is next year's head start.

## Templates

### Diwali announcement (WhatsApp, Hinglish)
```
🪔 Diwali Dhamaka — <Shop Name> 🪔
Is Diwali, <main offer — e.g. Sarees aur Suits par 20% OFF + Free Gift Packing>
📅 Offer: <start date> se <end date> tak
📍 <Shop address> | 📞 <Phone>
Aap sabko Diwali ki dher saari shubhkamnayein! 🙏
```

### Eid greeting + offer (WhatsApp, respectful)
```
🌙 Eid Mubarak! — <Shop Name>
Aapko aur aapke parivaar ko Eid ki dil se mubarakbaad.
Eid ke khaas mauke par: <main offer — e.g. Kurta-Pyjama aur Sherwani par 15% OFF>
📅 <dates> tak | 📍 <address>
```

### Rakhi — gift shop (Hinglish)
```
🎀 Rakhi Special — <Shop Name>
Behen ke liye mithaas bhara tohfa: <offer — e.g. Rakhi + Mithaas Combo ₹299 se shuru>
Rakhi wale din tak home delivery uplabdh hai 🚚
Order ke liye isi number par message karein 🙏
```

### Thank-you after purchase (any festival)
```
Dhanyavaad <Customer Name> ji 🙏
<Shop Name> se <festival> ki khareed ke liye shukriya.
Koi bhi zaroorat ho to bas ek message kar dijiyega — hum hamesha yahi hain.
— <Shop Name>, <City>
```

## Rules & common mistakes
- ❌ Announcing the offer on the festival day itself — by then customers have bought elsewhere. Start 2–3 weeks ahead.
- ❌ Wrong or careless greetings: learn the correct greeting for the festival (Eid Mubarak, Happy Onam, Shubh Deepavali) and never mix two festivals in one message.
- ❌ Discount so deep that the festival week runs at a loss — plan the margin first, the banner second.
- ❌ Spamming the same list daily — 3–4 well-timed messages beat 15 random ones, and angry customers block the number.
- ❌ Forgetting stock: advertising an offer on an item that finishes on day one destroys trust.
- ✅ Respect every festival your customers celebrate, including ones you don't — that respect is the real long-term marketing.
- ✅ This skill helps with planning and messages only. Any claim in an ad (price, discount, "lowest price") must be true for your shop — the business owner is responsible for it.
