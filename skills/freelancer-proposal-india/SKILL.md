# Freelancer Proposal India

For Indian freelancers — website developers, designers, SEO writers, video
editors, social media managers — who need to send a client a short, professional
proposal that says clearly what will be done, what it costs, when it will be
delivered, and how payment works. No legal jargon, no long contract — one page
a client can say yes to.

> General information only — not legal or tax advice. A proposal is not a
> contract; for large projects, get the final terms in writing (email or a
> simple agreement) before starting work.

## When to use this skill
- "Client ko proposal bhejna hai" / "Quotation banao"
- "Freelance rate kya rakhu?" / "Is kaam ka kitna charge karu?"
- "Advance kitna lena chahiye?" / milestone payment terms
- "Scope likhna hai" — what is included and what is not
- "Client ne revision maangi hai" / extra work beyond the quote
- "Payment late ho raha hai" — polite follow-up

## What you need from the user
- Your service: website, design, SEO, content, video, social media
- Client name and business (shop, company, startup)
- What the client wants, in their words — pages, posts, keywords, videos
- Your price: total project fee, or monthly retainer, or hourly rate
- Timeline: start date and delivery date
- Payment method you accept: UPI, bank transfer, or both

Never ask for or store the client's Aadhaar, PAN, bank account number,
passwords or OTPs. A proposal needs only names, scope, amounts and dates.

## Workflow

### 1. Write the scope first, price second
A proposal fails when the scope is vague — "website bana dunga" leads to
endless extra pages later. Write the scope as a short list the client can
tick off:

- Exactly what will be delivered (e.g. 5-page website, home + about +
  services + contact + blog setup)
- What is included (e.g. mobile-friendly design, basic on-page SEO,
  contact form, 1 month of small fixes after delivery)
- What is NOT included (e.g. logo design, paid ads, content writing
  beyond 500 words per page, hosting charges)
- How many revision rounds are included (usually 2 — see step 4)

If the client asks for something outside this list later, it is extra work
with an extra price — say so politely at that time, not after doing it free.

### 2. Price it three ways — and pick one
- **Fixed project fee** — best for a defined job like a website or logo.
  Quote one total; the client knows the full cost upfront.
- **Monthly retainer** — best for ongoing work like SEO, social media or
  maintenance. Quote a per-month fee and what it covers each month.
- **Hourly rate** — best for small or unclear jobs. Quote the rate and an
  estimated number of hours, with a maximum cap so the client is not
  surprised.

Never quote only a daily or hourly rate for a big defined job — the client
cannot tell what the total will be, and you cannot tell when it ends.

### 3. Set milestone payments — always take an advance
Indian freelance work runs on milestones. Never start without an advance.

- **Fixed project:** 50% advance to start, 30% on design/content approval,
  20% on final delivery. For small jobs under ₹10,000, 50% advance and
  50% on delivery is fine.
- **Monthly retainer:** full month's fee in advance, before the month's
  work starts — or at least 50% in advance and 50% by the 15th.
- **Hourly work:** weekly billing, payment within 7 days of the invoice.

Write the payment method in the proposal: UPI ID or bank account details
go in the invoice, not in the proposal itself. And write the late-payment
rule once: "Payment due within 7 days of invoice; work pauses if a
milestone payment is more than 7 days late."

### 4. Fix the revision rule in writing
Most freelancer-client fights are about revisions. Put this line in every
proposal:

- 2 revision rounds included after each delivery stage (design, then
  final). A revision round means a list of changes sent together, not
  one change at a time over weeks.
- After 2 rounds, further changes are charged — hourly rate or a fixed
  fee per round, stated in the proposal.
- Revision means changing what was already agreed; adding a new page,
  a new section or a new feature is new scope, not a revision.

### 5. Add GST only if you are registered
- If you are **not GST-registered**, quote the fee as-is and say so:
  "GST not applicable."
- If you **are GST-registered**, add 18% GST on professional services
  and show it as a separate line — fee + GST = total payable. Never
  hide GST inside the fee and discover it later.
- If the client is a business, they may ask for a GST invoice — only
  promise one if you are registered and can issue it.

### 6. Close with a validity date and a yes/no step
Every proposal ends with two lines:

- "This quotation is valid for 15 days from the date above."
- "Reply 'OK' on WhatsApp/email to confirm, and I will share the UPI
  details for the advance."

A proposal without a validity date stays open forever and the price
gets negotiated months later. A proposal without a clear next step
gets read and forgotten.

## Worked example 1 — website project, fixed fee
Client: a clinic in Malegaon wants a 5-page website. Freelancer quotes
₹60,000 fixed, delivery in 3 weeks, 2 revision rounds included.

- Advance (50%) to start: **₹30,000**
- On design approval (30%): **₹18,000**
- On final delivery (20%): **₹12,000**
- Check: 30,000 + 18,000 + 12,000 = **₹60,000** ✓
- If GST-registered: GST 18% on ₹60,000 = **₹10,800**, so the client
  pays ₹60,000 + ₹10,800 = **₹70,800** in total. Show both lines.
- If the client adds a 6th page later: that is new scope — quote it
  separately (e.g. ₹5,000) instead of doing it free.

## Worked example 2 — monthly SEO retainer
Client: a shop wants on-page SEO and 4 blog posts a month. Retainer
₹25,000 per month, 3-month minimum.

- Month 1 fee in advance: **₹25,000** (before work starts)
- 3 months total: 25,000 × 3 = **₹75,000**
- What it covers each month: 4 blog posts, on-page SEO for 5 pages,
  monthly report on WhatsApp. What it does not cover: paid ads budget,
  website redesign.
- If the client wants an hourly job instead: 40 hours × ₹800/hour =
  **₹32,000** — quote the hours and the cap in writing.

## Proposal template

```
PROPOSAL / QUOTATION — <Your Name / Business Name>
Date: <Date> | Valid for 15 days
To: <Client Name> — <Business Name>

WHAT I WILL DO (Scope)
1. <Deliverable 1 — e.g. 5-page mobile-friendly website>
2. <Deliverable 2 — e.g. basic on-page SEO + contact form>
3. <Deliverable 3 — e.g. 1 month of small fixes after delivery>

NOT INCLUDED
- <e.g. Logo design, hosting charges, paid ads>

PRICE
Total fee: ₹<...> (GST: <Not applicable / +18% = ₹...>)
Total payable: ₹<...>

PAYMENT (Milestones)
- 50% advance to start: ₹<...>
- 30% on approval: ₹<...>
- 20% on delivery: ₹<...>
Payment by UPI / bank transfer. Due within 7 days of invoice.

REVISIONS
2 revision rounds included per stage. Extra rounds charged at ₹<...>
per round. New pages/features are new scope, quoted separately.

TIMELINE
Start: <Date — after advance received> | Delivery: <Date>

To confirm, reply "OK" and I will share payment details.
— <Your Name> | <Phone / WhatsApp> | <Email>
```

## Rules & common mistakes
- ❌ Starting work without an advance — the commonest way freelancers
  lose money in India.
- ❌ Quoting a price without writing the scope — "website" means 5
  pages to you and 15 pages to the client.
- ❌ Unlimited revisions — always cap them at 2 rounds per stage.
- ❌ Doing extra work free and billing later — quote new scope before
  doing it, not after.
- ❌ Hiding GST until the invoice — state it in the proposal or state
  that it does not apply.
- ❌ No validity date — a quote from 3 months ago is not today's price.
- ❌ Sending bank details in the proposal — payment details go in the
  invoice, after the client says yes.
- ✅ Get the client's "OK" in writing (WhatsApp or email) before
  starting — a chat message is enough to confirm scope and price.
- ✅ Keep one rate card for your common jobs (website, SEO month,
  logo, video) so every quote takes 10 minutes, not a day.
- ✅ This skill writes proposals and quotations; it is not a legal
  contract. For large projects, put the final terms in a simple written
  agreement.
