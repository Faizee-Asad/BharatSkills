# DPDP Basics India

For a small business, shop, clinic, school, freelancer or website owner in
India who collects customer names, phone numbers, emails or addresses —
on paper, on WhatsApp, in a spreadsheet, or on a website form. It turns
"DPDP ka darr" into a one-page checklist: what you collect, why, how long
you keep it, and how you delete it when asked.

> General information only — not legal advice. The law is new and the
> dates below are as notified; always check the official text (MeitY /
> Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025) or ask a
> lawyer before relying on any line in this skill for your business.

## The dates that matter (verified, November 2026)

- **11 August 2023** — Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
  (DPDP Act) enacted.
- **13 November 2025** — DPDP Rules, 2025 notified. The Data Protection
  Board of India provisions started.
- **13 November 2026** — Consent Manager registration framework started.
- **13 May 2027** — the main duties on businesses start: notice and
  consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, retention and erasure,
  children's data, rights and grievances. That is about **6 months** from
  now (November 2026) — the time to fix your data habits, not to panic.

Nothing in this skill is enforceable against a small business today, but
the date is fixed. A shop that cleans up its customer list now will not
have to do it in a rush in May 2027.

## What you need from the user
- What kind of business it is (shop, clinic, school, website, freelancer)
- What personal data it collects: name, phone, email, address, photo,
  payment details — and where it sits (register, WhatsApp, Excel/Google
  Sheet, website form, billing software)
- Why it collects each item (order delivery, offers, fees, job work)
- Who else sees the data (staff, accountant, delivery boy, software)

Never ask for or store any real customer's Aadhaar, PAN, bank account,
password or OTP. A DPDP checklist needs the *types* of data, never the
data itself.

## The 4 words to learn first

- **Personal data** — any data about a person you can identify from it:
  name + phone number, email, address, photo. The Act has no separate
  "sensitive data" category — all personal data gets the same duties.
- **Data Principal** — the person the data is about: your customer,
  employee, student, website visitor. For a child, it includes the
  parent or lawful guardian.
- **Data Fiduciary** — whoever decides *why* and *how* the data is used:
  that is you, the business. You carry most of the duties.
- **Data Processor** — someone who handles data on your behalf: your
  billing software, cloud host, payroll provider, delivery partner.

The Act covers **digital** personal data — collected on a computer, or
collected on paper and later typed into one. Paper that stays paper is
outside it. There is **no size limit**: a shop with 200 customer numbers
is covered just like a big company (the government may exempt some
classes, like start-ups, from some duties — check before assuming).

## Workflow

### 1. Make your data list (30 minutes, once)
One row per data type. No row, no collection.

```
DATA — <Business name> — <Date>
What we collect | Why we need it | Where it sits | Who can see it | Delete after
Name + phone    | Order + delivery | WhatsApp + register | Owner + 1 staff | 12 months no purchase
Email           | Bill + offers    | Billing software    | Owner           | 12 months no purchase
Address         | Home delivery    | Order book          | Delivery boy    | After delivery + 30 days
Staff salary    | Pay + PF record  | Excel (locked)      | Owner + CA      | As per law / 8 years*
```

\* Salary/PF records have their own legal retention — keep what the
other law needs, delete the rest. If you do not know the period, ask
your CA; do not guess and do not keep it forever.

Count the places your data sits. Most small businesses find 4–6:
register, phone, WhatsApp, spreadsheet, billing app, website form.
Every extra copy is a leak waiting to happen.

### 2. Check your ground: consent or legitimate use
Personal data needs a ground. For most small businesses it is **consent**:

- Consent must be **free, specific, informed and unambiguous** — the
  person knows what data, for what purpose, and says yes clearly.
- A pre-ticked box, a hidden line in terms, or "number diya to matlab
  haan" is **not** consent.
- Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it — one message,
  "messages band karo", and you stop.
- Before or with consent, give a **notice**: what data, why, and how to
  withdraw / complain. Notice in English or any language in the Eighth
  Schedule of the Constitution (Hindi counts).

The other ground is **legitimate use** — a short list in the Act, for
example using employee data for employment purposes, or responding to a
medical emergency. It does not cover marketing. If you are unsure which
ground applies, treat it as consent and ask.

### 3. Fix the three places data leaks most
- **WhatsApp groups / broadcasts:** never add a number to an offer
  broadcast unless that person agreed to offers. One "stop" message =
  remove the same day, and confirm it.
- **Bought number lists:** do not buy phone-number lists and do not
  message them. No consent, no ground — and the first complaint names
  your business.
- **Shared phones and sheets:** one staff phone with all customer chats
  is one lost phone away from a breach. Keep business data on the
  business phone/account, lock the sheet, and do not forward customer
  lists to personal numbers.

### 4. Set retention, then actually delete
Keep data only as long as the purpose needs it:

- Offer list: delete numbers with **no purchase in 12 months**.
- Enquiry forms: delete non-customers after **90 days**.
- Delivery addresses: delete **30 days after delivery**.
- Ex-employee personal data (phone, address): delete when the job ends,
  except what tax/PF law requires you to keep.

Put one date in the calendar every month: "purana data delete karo".
Deletion is a duty, not a favour.

### 5. Children's data — the strictest rule
A child is anyone **under 18**. For a child's data you need **verifiable
consent from the parent or lawful guardian** — not the child's own yes.
And for children, no tracking, no behavioural monitoring, and no
targeted advertising. Schools and coaching centres: collect only what
admission and safety need, and keep it away from marketing lists.

### 6. When someone asks: access, correction, deletion
A Data Principal can ask for: a summary of their data, correction of
wrong data, erasure of their data, and a way to complain (grievance
redressal). They can also **nominate** someone to act for them if they
die or cannot act.

Your reply, every time, in 3 steps:
1. Confirm who they are (one check — the same phone/email the data
   came from — never ask for an ID document over chat).
2. Do it: correct, delete, or send the summary — and tell them it is
   done.
3. Note it in one line (date, request, done) so the same request twice
   gets the same answer.

### 7. If data leaks — the breach plan
A breach is any unauthorised access, loss or leak — a lost phone with
customer chats, a sheet shared to the wrong group, a hacked account.

1. **Stop it:** change the password, remove the share, lock the account.
2. **Note it:** what leaked, whose data, how many people, when you
   found out. Write it the same day — memory fades, the note does not.
3. **Tell without delay:** inform the Data Protection Board and the
   affected people. "Without delay" means do not sit on it for a week.
4. **Fix the cause:** the shared password, the unlocked sheet, the
   ex-staff member who still has access — remove access the same day.

Penalties under the Act can go up to **₹250 crore** for serious breaches
of duty — the number is a ceiling for the worst cases, not a fine every
small shop will face. The point is not the number; it is that "humein
pata hi nahi chala" is not a defence.

## Worked example 1 — a shop's offer list, in numbers
A kirana / mobile shop takes 50 new customer numbers a week for offers.

- Numbers in a year = 50 × 52 = **2,600**
- After 12 months, about 40% never bought again: 2,600 × 40% = **1,040**
  numbers to delete at the yearly clean-up.
- Kept list after clean-up = 2,600 − 1,040 = **1,560** real customers.
- Staff time: deleting 1,040 rows from one sheet takes under an hour,
  once a year. Keeping them risks complaints from 1,040 people who
  forgot they ever gave their number.

## Worked example 2 — a website's enquiry form, in numbers
A service website gets 30 enquiries a month.

- Enquiries in a year = 30 × 12 = **360**
- About 20% become customers: 360 × 20% = **72** customers to keep.
- The other 360 − 72 = **288** enquiries a year are non-customers —
  delete them 90 days after the enquiry.
- One rule in the form tool ("auto-delete after 90 days") does this
  without anyone remembering. If the tool cannot auto-delete, put a
  monthly calendar reminder.

## Worked example 3 — "mera data delete karo" (WhatsApp)
Customer Ramesh, who bought once 8 months ago, messages: "Mera number
delete karo, offers mat bhejo."

1. Check: the number matches the order book — same person, no ID asked.
2. Delete the number from the offer broadcast list **and** the sheet,
   the same day. Keep the bill copy if tax law needs it — the bill is a
   legal record, the marketing number is not.
3. Reply: "Ho gaya — aapka number offer list se hata diya hai. Aage se
   koi offer message nahi aayega." (template below)
4. Note one line: "12 Nov 2026 — Ramesh — delete request — done."

## Templates

### A. WhatsApp consent message (Hinglish, copy-ready)

```
Namaste! <Shop name> se order ke liye dhanyavad. 🙏

Aapka number hum sirf in kaamo ke liye rakhenge:
• Order aur delivery update
• Bill bhejna
• Offers — sirf agar aap haan bole

Offers nahi chahiye to abhi "STOP" likh bhejiye — number turant
hata diya jayega. Aapka number kisi aur ko nahi diya jayega.

Haan bolne ke liye "HAAN" likh bhejiye.
```

### B. Website / paper-form notice (English, copy-ready)

```
What we collect and why — <Business name>

We collect your name, phone number and address only to:
• Deliver your order and send your bill
• Contact you about your order
• Send offers — only if you tick "Yes, send me offers"

We keep your details for 12 months after your last purchase, then
delete them. You can ask us to correct or delete your details, or
stop offers, any time: message us on <WhatsApp number> or email
<email>. We do not sell or share your details with anyone except
delivery and payment partners who need them for your order.
```

### C. Reply to a delete / stop request

```
Ho gaya — aapka number offer list se hata diya hai. Aage se koi
offer message nahi aayega. Aapka order/bill record kanoon ke hisaab
se alag se rakha jayega, marketing ke liye use nahi hoga.
```

### D. Breach note (fill the same day)

```
BREACH NOTE — <Date> <Time>
What happened: <lost phone / wrong share / hacked account>
Whose data: <e.g. customer names + phone numbers>
How many people: <count or best estimate>
How we stopped it: <password changed / share removed / ...>
Who we told: <Board + affected people — date/time>
What we fixed so it does not happen again: <...>
```

## Rules & common mistakes
- ❌ Collecting Aadhaar, PAN or bank details "just in case" — if the
  purpose does not need it, do not collect it.
- ❌ Pre-ticked "send me offers" boxes, or consent hidden in long terms.
- ❌ Buying phone-number lists, or adding numbers from a visiting card
  to a broadcast without asking.
- ❌ Keeping ex-customer and ex-employee data forever "kyunki kabhi kaam
  aa jaye" — retention without a purpose is the breach.
- ❌ One password for the business Gmail, billing app and WhatsApp —
  one leak opens everything.
- ❌ Telling a customer "data delete nahi ho sakta" — correction and
  erasure are their rights; the bill record is the only usual exception.
- ❌ Children's photos, school lists or tuition numbers used for ads —
  no targeted ads at children, ever.
- ✅ One data list, one retention rule, one monthly delete date.
- ✅ Consent in the customer's own language, one line, before marketing.
- ✅ "STOP" = stop the same day, and confirm it in one message.
- ✅ A breach note written the same day beats a perfect memory a week
  later.

## Significant Data Fiduciary — probably not you
The government can notify very large data handlers as Significant Data
Fiduciaries, who then have extra duties: a Data Protection Officer,
independent audits, and impact assessments. A small shop, clinic or
school will almost certainly not be notified. If you are ever notified,
that is the day to get a lawyer — not before.
